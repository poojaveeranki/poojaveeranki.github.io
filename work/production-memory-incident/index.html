Root-causing a production memory exhaustion incident

Tracing service failures back to a document-conversion fallback process that consumed memory until other services stopped, and escalating to the vendor.

  • Incident
  • Logs
  • Vendor escalation

Context

A production node of the Intelligent Viewing platform ran out of memory one evening. Within two minutes the configuration, markup and publisher services had stopped, and document publishing was unavailable. The services did not recover on their own.

I investigated the incident, wrote up the root cause, and escalated it to the vendor.

What I did

  • Read the application logs for the publisher and the other services around the time of the failure.
  • Correlated them with the Windows event log, which recorded an out-of-memory event at the moment the services stopped.
  • Traced the memory consumption back to a single process and reconstructed the sequence that led to it.
  • Prepared the incident report and the corrective actions.
  • Raised a high-priority support case with OpenText and pressed for a product-level fix rather than a cleanup.

What went wrong and how it was found

The chain of events, as the logs showed it:

  1. The publisher tried to convert an Office document with its built-in converter.
  2. The built-in converter could not handle the document, so the publisher fell back to LibreOffice.
  3. One DOCX file failed in LibreOffice four times within a few minutes.
  4. After the failures, a LibreOffice process was left behind and kept running.
  5. That process kept allocating memory until it reached roughly 50 GB.
  6. Windows recorded the out-of-memory event.
  7. The other services could no longer obtain memory and stopped.
  8. Nothing restarted them automatically.

The immediate cause was the orphaned conversion process. The underlying cause was that a failed fallback conversion did not clean up after itself, and that is a product behaviour, not a configuration mistake.

Corrective actions

  • Identified and removed the leftover LibreOffice processes on both production nodes.
  • Planned checks for orphaned conversion processes as part of routine operations.
  • Escalated to OpenText support with the evidence, asking for a fix in the product.

What I learned

The visible symptom, services stopping, was three steps away from the cause, a document that one converter could not handle. Working backwards through the logs rather than restarting and hoping was what made the vendor conversation productive.
